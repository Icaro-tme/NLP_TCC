The exposure limits of Annex I to NR-15 refers to continuous or intermittent noise. Continuous noise is what remains stable with maximum variations of 3 to 5 dB(A) for a long period. Intermittent noise is a noise with larger or lesser variations of intensity. Impact noise is what presents peak with a duration less than 1 second, at intervals greater than 1 second. The NR-15 itself clarifies that It is understood by Continuous or Intermittent noise, for the purposes of application of Tolerance Limits, the noise that is not impact noise, according to item 1 of Annex I.

In this sense, intermittent is that noise that is not presented uniformly during the working day, transiting in varied levels, above or below the tolerance limit and therefore requiring a definition of average value, pressed through noise dosimetria . The Federal Law Interpretation Request was filed by the author against the judgment of the 3rd Court of Appeal of São Paulo which confirmed the judgment of improcedence of the request for pension grant for time of contribution, through the recognition of special activity. The dispute involves various working periods in which the author claims exposure to unhealthy conditions, notably noise, and exercise of the driver's function in special conditions.

The appealed judgment understood that the documents submitted did not prove the speciality of the labor, highlighting the absence of evidence on the type of vehicle driven, irregularity in the PPPs for lack of technical manager and exposure to noise below or equal to the limit of legal tolerance.

There are five questions being discussed: (i) knowing whether the framework of the driver’s activity as special is possible based only on the note of the function in the CTPS and in the company’s branch of activity; (ii) knowing whether the absence of opportunity for proof production when the absence of the indication of the technical manager in the PPPs characterizes defence certification and invalidates the proof of speciality; (iii) knowing whether the exposure to noise within the exact limit of 85 dB(A) is sufficient for the recognition of the activity as special; (iv) knowing whether the mention of the intermittent exposure to noise removes the recognition of the speciality; and (v) knowing whether the PUIL can be known when the recurrence in jurisdiction is in accordance with the RAZID III.

4. The application for uniformization is not known when the appealed judgment is aligned with TNU’s dominant jurisprudence, in accordance with Order Question 13/TNU. 5. As regards the framework of the driver’s activity, the consolidated jurisprudence of TNU requires proof of the type of vehicle driven, being not sufficient the general note of the function in the CTPS, although accompanied by information about the branch of the company. The absence of a technical manager in the PPPs makes it impossible to accept the document as proof of special activity, as established in the Thesis 208/TNU. The claim of defense certification is not sufficient for admission of the PUIL, in the terms of the Summary 43/TNU.

The recognition of the specialty due to exposure to noise depends on the exceeding of the limit of tolerance set in the current legislation at the time.
